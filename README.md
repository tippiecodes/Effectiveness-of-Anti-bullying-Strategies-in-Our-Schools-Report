# Effectiveness of Anti-bullying Strategies in Our Schools

A statistical research project examining whether anti-bullying strategies such as curriculum-based education, reporting systems, bystander training and digital citizenship effectively reduce bullying and cyberbullying incidents among youths.

## Project Overview

This Statistical Research Methods (IT2238) report investigates whether anti-bullying strategies implemented in schools are effective in reducing bullying and cyberbullying incidents among youths. The project is based on survey data collected from 91 respondents and applies binomial distribution, linear regression and thematic analysis of qualitative responses. The study evaluates four key intervention areas to understand how different strategies influence student behaviour, perceptions of safety and online conduct.

## Key Findings

• Anti-bullying education contributes to higher awareness, increased empathy and more respectful interactions among students.  
• Most respondents feel safe in their school environments yet remain unfamiliar with existing reporting procedures.  
• A limited proportion of students receive bystander training, leading to lower confidence and a higher likelihood of worsening situations when intervening.  
• Digital citizenship education is strongly associated with meaningful improvements in online behaviour, with a substantial proportion of trained respondents reporting positive changes.

## Conclusion

The findings support the conclusion that comprehensive anti-bullying strategies are effective in reducing both bullying and cyberbullying incidents among youths. While current measures contribute positively to safety and awareness, further improvements can be achieved by increasing student access to training, improving familiarity with reporting systems and sustaining emphasis on preventive education and digital literacy.
